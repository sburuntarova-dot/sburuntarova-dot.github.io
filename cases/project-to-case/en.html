01Overview

How do you help a student turn a project into a case study they’re proud to show?

Art Glück students make strong projects, but in their portfolios they turn into a folder of renders. I went the whole way on my own, from the first research interviews to the release: I talked to students, came up with the flow, designed the interface and templates, and launched the site with AI tools.

02Problem

Strong student projects end up as weak portfolio case studies.

The students’ renders are great. But a case study is a story: the brief, the idea, the decisions. Between a folder of images and that story there’s a blank page — and that’s where everything stops.

03Research

I talked to the three most active students in the school.

I wanted to understand why even they had no case studies. The answers weren’t about design.

It’s not the design that gets in the way — it’s not knowing what to say.

So the product doesn’t need yet another pretty template. What it needs is to ask the right questions — one at a time, in the same order in which the project’s story comes together.

04Solution

Project → Case: questions instead of a blank page.

The student goes through six short steps: answers simple questions about the project and uploads images. The builder turns the answers into a story, picks a template and gives a link to the finished case study — ready to send to an employer.

06Exploration

I made four different layouts — and the student chooses one.

A case has to work for any project: with three images or thirty, with long answers or short ones. The builder looks at the material and recommends a layout, but any of them can be chosen at any time, even after the case is published.

We recommend, the student decides.

Every layout holds three images or twenty; the student’s answers read as a story, not as captions to renders. And the layout is made of simple blocks — easy to put together automatically.

07Decisions

The builder doesn’t leave the student alone when something goes wrong.

Too few images, a file that didn’t upload, a build that failed — each case has a clear explanation and a next step. Answers are never lost. And a case only goes into the portfolio after a curator has reviewed it carefully and approved it for publishing.
