01Problem

Every study track lived by its own set of visual rules.

Art Glück is an online school of interior design and architecture with 6 study tracks and 15+ software courses. Its visuals were built on bright illustrations, and over the years every track grew its own colour and illustration style — and as the school kept growing, keeping the brand recognisable across every channel got harder and harder.

02Direction

The illustrations pulled focus from what matters most — the students’ work.

Student work is a strong picture on its own, and the bright illustrated style competed with it.

So the answer wasn’t to “draw a prettier illustration” but to remove the extra visual noise altogether.

I chose a neutral system: a restrained pastel palette and minimal 3D icons instead of a mismatched set of illustrations, one for every track. This approach doesn’t compete with the students’ work — it frames it: the school’s visuals became a backdrop for the results rather than a statement of their own, competing for attention.

03Identity

A logo and a palette that work in any context, on any background.

Four contrasting logo versions — on light, dark and colour backgrounds and over photos. A palette on a neutral base with pastel accents, and blue as the only bright colour for graphics.

04Website

With the team, we rebuilt the website around the new structure and system.

I designed a new site structure for the 6 study tracks and built the brand system into the front end together with the developer.

The school launches new courses all the time, and designing a unique page for each one doesn’t scale. So instead of one-off layouts we built a standard landing page template: a new course is put together by swapping content, not by rebuilding the layout.

05Merch

Merch people actually want to wear, not hide in a closet.

I carried the brand system onto physical items: tote bags, badges, caps, T-shirts and stickers. The logo and pastel palette work on fabric and on plastic alike — the brand is recognisable off screen too.

06Solution

The whole system — from the logo to an ad banner.

07Results

One system made the brand recognisable across every channel.

A set of ~25 ad creatives that used to take 2–3 days to put together by hand now takes about 6 hours. But the main win is the team’s independence: designers solve tasks on their own with the brand book, without signing off every step.
